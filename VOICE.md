# How this site talks

Read this before writing or editing any words on the site. It applies to people and to AI.

## Where this comes from

Chris, on 2026-09-30:

> "We need my smart ass lovable sense of humor to stand out throughout the entire site. Make people feel warm and welcome but also fun and laughing, because yes, I'm laughing at myself, because this is probably fine, right? ... I'm a character and love to have fun but still being 1000% professional."

In one line: **a smart-ass you would actually want to work with.**

## The rules

1. **Chris is the butt of the joke.** Never the visitor, a client, a competitor, or anyone else.
2. **The facts don't bend.** What a product does, what is shipped, numbers, dates, and job titles are stated straight. A joke can sit next to a fact; it can't replace one or stretch one.
3. **Warm first, funny second.** The reader should feel welcome before they feel amused. Talk to them like someone who just walked into the shop.
4. **Short and dry.** Plain words, short sentences, and the occasional aside in parentheses. The aside is where the joke lives.
5. **"Probably fine" is the running gag, so ration it.** The studio is named after its quality-assurance process. Once per page section is plenty.
6. **Go plain where trust is on the line.** The resume, what a product actually does, and how to get in touch are clear before they are clever. Nobody should have to decode a joke to find information.
7. **The studio started solo and is looking to grow.** Never call it a "solo studio" or a "one-person studio" as its identity. It is one person *so far*.
8. **Sign off with "Cheers!"** It is how Chris actually ends his messages.

## Does not sound like Chris

| Instead of | Write |
| :--- | :--- |
| "We deliver innovative digital solutions." | "I build things until they work." |
| "Passionate about leveraging cutting-edge AI." | "AI does the heavy lifting. I decide whether it's any good." |
| "Reach out to explore synergies." | "Come say hi." |
| A joke about the visitor's bad idea | A joke about Chris's 45 repos |
| Three jokes in one paragraph | One, and then the facts |

## Where the jokes go, and where they don't

| Place | How much |
| :--- | :--- |
| Homepage, About, 404, footer, section intros | Plenty. This is where the character shows. |
| Project descriptions | A dry line is welcome, after the sentence that says what the thing is. |
| Case-study facts, highlights, tech stacks | None. These are the receipts. |
| Resume | Straight. (The page says so itself: "The jokes are on the other pages.") |
| Contact form | Light. Never in the way of sending a message. |

## Before you publish a line

- Is it true? If it states something about Chris, a product, or a date, can you point to where that came from?
- Who is the joke on? If the answer is not "Chris" or "the process", cut it.
- Would it still make sense to someone who has never heard of the studio?
- Did you just use "probably fine" for the third time on this page?
